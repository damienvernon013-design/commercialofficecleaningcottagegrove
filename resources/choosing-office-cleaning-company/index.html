How to Choose an Office Cleaning Company in the South Metro

There are a lot of cleaning contractors in the Twin Cities south metro. Most of them will give you a reasonable quote. The question is what happens six months in when the crew turns over, the scope gets informal, and the invoice doesn't match what was agreed to. Here is what to look for before you sign.

Verify Insurance Before You Do Anything Else

A cleaning contractor who is not insured creates liability for your organization. If a crew member is injured in your facility, or if something is damaged or stolen, you need their coverage to respond — not yours. Before any contractor starts work in your building, request a current certificate of insurance and verify that it names your organization as certificate holder.

A contractor who cannot produce a certificate quickly, or who produces one with a lapsed date, is not insured for practical purposes regardless of what they claim verbally. Do not accept verbal assurances. Get the document.

Require a Written Scope

A verbal agreement about what will be cleaned is not an agreement that either party will hold to six months from now. Every contractor you consider should be willing to produce a written scope of work that specifies tasks, frequencies, and exclusions for your specific facility. If they offer only a standard package, ask them to modify it to reflect your space. If they cannot or will not do that, move on.

The scope is also how you evaluate competing quotes. Two quotes that look similar in total price may differ significantly in what they include. A lower quote that excludes restroom restocking or weekly floor mopping is not a lower price for equivalent service.

Ask About Crew Consistency

The most common complaint facility managers have about cleaning contractors is crew turnover. A contractor that assigns consistent, named crew members to your account is worth more than one that dispatches whoever is available. Ask directly: "Who specifically will clean our office, and what happens if that person is not available?" The answer tells you a great deal about how the contractor actually operates.

Check for Local Presence and South Metro Experience

A contractor based in the south metro who already serves accounts along your corridor is more likely to staff your account consistently than one routing crews from across the metro. Ask where their other accounts in your area are, and whether they are familiar with Washington and Dakota County winter conditions — road salt management is a specific and important issue for hard-floor offices in this part of Minnesota.

Evaluate the Quote Process

A contractor who quotes your facility without a walkthrough is pricing an imaginary average office, not yours. Any quote worth taking seriously should come after a walkthrough. If the contractor will not schedule a walkthrough for a standard commercial account, that tells you something about how they will treat the account after they have it.

Questions to Ask

  • Can you provide a certificate of insurance naming my organization as certificate holder?
  • Will you produce a written scope specific to our facility before we sign?
  • Who are the crew members assigned to our account?
  • What is your process if a scheduled visit is missed or incomplete?
  • Do you have existing accounts in Cottage Grove, Woodbury, or the surrounding area?
  • How do you handle winter salt management for hard-floor entries?
