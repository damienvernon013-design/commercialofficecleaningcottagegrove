Cleaning Considerations for Shared-Equipment Facilities

Any facility where multiple people share equipment throughout the day — conference room technology, breakroom appliances, print stations, hot-desking workstations — faces a hygiene challenge that private, single-user spaces don't: constant hand contact with the same surfaces by different people.

Why Shared Equipment Needs Its Own Protocol

A shared keyboard, a breakroom microwave door, or a conference-room touchscreen gets touched by many different hands over the course of a day. Standard nightly cleaning addresses visible dirt, but high-touch shared equipment benefits from more frequent attention.

What to Prioritize

Shared workstations and hot desks: keyboards, mice, and desk surfaces used by rotating staff should be wiped down between users where practical, and disinfected as part of the regular cleaning visit. Breakroom appliances: microwave door handles, refrigerator handles, and coffee station surfaces see constant multi-person contact. Conference room equipment: touchscreens, remote controls, and table surfaces used across back-to-back meetings.

What to Ask a Provider

Ask whether your cleaning company's standard scope already covers shared equipment as a distinct line item, or whether it needs to be added explicitly. A scope that only says “wipe down surfaces” may not specifically include shared electronics or appliance touchpoints.
