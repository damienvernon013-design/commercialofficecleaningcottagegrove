What to Look for in an Office Cleaning Contract

A commercial cleaning contract is a legal document that defines your service relationship. Yet many business owners sign them after a quick skim, focusing on the price and missing the clauses that determine what actually happens when things go wrong.

The Essential Sections

Scope of work: this is the most important section. It should itemize every cleaning task, specify the areas covered, and define the frequency for each task. Service frequency and schedule: the contract should state clearly how often service occurs and on which days. Pricing and payment terms: confirm whether pricing is monthly or per-visit, and when invoices are issued. See our pricing page for how we structure ours. Contract duration and renewal: most commercial cleaning contracts run for a set term and auto-renew unless cancelled within a notice window. Termination clause: what does it cost to exit early? Complaint and remedy process: how do you report a problem, and what's the response time commitment? Insurance and liability: the contract should confirm the company carries appropriate insurance — see our insured and bonded page.

What to Negotiate

Contracts are starting points, not take-it-or-leave-it documents. Ask for a trial period and performance benchmarks tied to your complaint resolution process. Get quotes from companies with transparent contracts at the quote form.
