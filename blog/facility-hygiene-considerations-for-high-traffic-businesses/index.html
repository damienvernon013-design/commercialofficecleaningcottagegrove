Facility Hygiene Considerations for High-Traffic Businesses

Businesses with high daily foot traffic — retail storefronts, multi-tenant lobbies, and public-facing service counters — face a hygiene challenge that a typical private office doesn't: constant turnover of visitors touching the same surfaces, tracking in debris, and generating more waste per square foot than a low-traffic space.

Why High Traffic Changes the Cleaning Math

A space with 200 visitors a day accumulates dirt, grime, and high-touch contamination at a different rate than a private office with 15 employees. Cleaning frequency, restroom servicing intervals, and entryway maintenance all need to scale with actual foot traffic, not just square footage.

What to Prioritize

Entryways and high-touch surfaces: door handles, point-of-sale counters, and shared surfaces near the entrance see the most contact and need the most frequent attention. Restroom servicing: public-facing restrooms used by dozens of people daily need more frequent checks than a private office restroom serving a handful of employees. Floor maintenance: higher foot traffic means faster wear and more frequent mopping or vacuuming, especially during Minnesota's winter salt season — see our winter floor care guide.

What to Ask a Provider

If your business sees significant daily foot traffic, ask any cleaning provider how their schedule adjusts for volume rather than just square footage, whether they can service restrooms multiple times a day if needed, and how they handle entryway upkeep during winter months.
