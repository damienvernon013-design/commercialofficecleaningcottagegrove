Cleaning Considerations for Shared Community Spaces

Multi-tenant buildings, shared lobbies, and common-use rooms present a different hygiene challenge than a single-tenant private office: the population using the space changes constantly, and no single occupant is responsible for its upkeep.

Why Shared Spaces Need a Different Approach

A private office's cleanliness is a direct reflection of one company. A shared lobby, elevator lobby, or common conference room serves everyone in the building and no one in particular — which means it's easy for standards to drift if cleaning isn't explicitly scoped and scheduled for that space.

What to Prioritize

High-touch shared surfaces: elevator buttons, door push plates, shared restroom fixtures, and lobby seating all see continuous multi-tenant contact. Common-area floor care: lobbies and shared corridors typically see more foot traffic than any single tenant's private space, and need floor maintenance scheduled accordingly — especially during Minnesota's winter salt season. Clear ownership: in a multi-tenant building, someone needs explicit responsibility for common areas. Without a scoped cleaning agreement, shared spaces can fall into a gap where every tenant assumes someone else is handling it.

What to Ask a Provider

If you manage or lease space in a multi-tenant building, ask whether your cleaning contract explicitly covers common areas separately from your private suite, and how frequently those shared spaces are serviced relative to your own space.
